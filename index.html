JARVIS

Your own Iron Man assistant, on your own Mac.

An always-listening voice assistant powered by Claude. Say “Hey Jarvis” and he answers, remembers, and gets things done.

Download for Mac

The app is free, but it runs on your own paid Claude plan (Pro or Max). Download is under 1 MB; first-time setup then fetches about 1.5 GB of speech models. About 10 minutes to set up. macOS 13 or newer.

What you need

Not free to run. Jarvis thinks with Claude Code, which needs a paid Claude Pro or Max subscription (claude.ai). Without one he cannot answer. Everything else in the download is free.
  • A Mac running macOS 13 or newerApple silicon is recommended; Intel works but transcribes more slowly.
  • A paid Claude account (Pro or Max)Setup installs Claude Code and asks you to sign in.
  • About 2 GB of free disk spaceIncludes the roughly 1.5 GB speech-model download on first setup.
  • An internet connection and a microphoneThe built-in mic is fine. Speakers or headphones for his voice.
  • About 10 minutesMostly waiting for the downloads.
  • Optional: Google ChromeThe glowing on-screen display opens there.

Set up in four steps

  1. Unzip and run setup. Unzip JARVIS-Mac.zip somewhere permanent (your home folder is fine). Open Terminal, type bash (with a space after it), drag setup.sh into the window, and press Return.

    It asks a few questions (your name, your city for weather), installs what it needs, and triggers the macOS permission prompts (microphone, Calendar, Reminders). Click Allow on each. The first run downloads about 1.5 GB of speech models, so leave it running until it finishes.

  2. Log in to Claude. If Claude Code opens during setup, sign in with your claude.ai account (the Pro or Max one), then type /exit.

  3. Connect your apps (optional). On claude.ai go to Settings › Connectors and connect Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar and Spotify. One time only, and you can connect only the ones you want.

  4. Say hello. Double-click Jarvis.command. The first time, macOS asks to let Terminal use the microphone: click Allow. Wait for “Jarvis online,” then say “Hey Jarvis.” On a brand-new install he gives a one-minute spoken welcome and offers a tour.

    To start him at login: System Settings › General › Login Items › + › Jarvis.command.

macOS may say the file is from an unidentified developer. Right-click Jarvis.command (or setup.sh), choose Open, then Open again.

Everything he does

Wake word and conversation

Say “Hey Jarvis,” wait for the little tink, and give your command. After he answers you can keep talking for about 7 seconds with no wake word.

  • Say “Hey Jarvis” while he is talking to cut him off.
  • “Never mind” or “that’s all” ends the conversation. “Start over” begins a fresh one. “Jarvis, shut down” quits.
  • He remembers the conversation, so follow-ups like “and what about Friday?” work.

Gmail

Read, search and summarize your inbox, draft replies, and send mail. He reads every message back to you and waits for your spoken “yes” before anything is sent. Needs the Gmail connector.

Calendar

“What’s on my calendar tomorrow?” and “Add lunch with Sam on Friday at noon.” The Mac’s own Calendar app works instantly with no setup beyond one Allow; the Google Calendar connector adds your Google calendars.

Google Drive

Find, open and read your documents and sheets, and summarize or quiz you on them. Needs the Google Drive connector.

Spotify

“Play some Miles Davis,” “What’s playing?”, pause, skip, build a playlist. Needs the Spotify connector, and the Spotify app open on your Mac.

Reminders and timers

  • Reminders go into the Mac’s Reminders app (so they reach your phone too). “Remind me to call the dentist at 4.”
  • Timers survive restarts, show a countdown on screen, and he chimes and speaks when they go off. “Set a timer for 20 minutes.”

The web and your Mac

He searches and reads the web (“Google who won the 1998 World Cup”), opens apps (“Open Calculator”), and can send an iMessage through the Messages app. Anything that sends, posts, buys or deletes waits for your spoken “yes” first.

Morning briefing

Say “good morning” or “brief me”: calendar, the last day of email, weather in your city, and reminders, spoken short with the details on screen. The first time you talk to him each morning he offers one.

The glowing display (HUD)

A holographic window opens in Chrome when Jarvis starts. It pulses while he listens and talks, shows lists, schedules, email drafts and progress tiles while he says the short version, and has a face that moves when he speaks. Turn it off with JARVIS_HUD=0.

Mavis and the rest of the cast

Mavis drops in to tease, argue and riff, and does research when he needs a second opinion. A few more characters come when you ask for them by name: ABG, Mario and Felix, each with their own voice and personality. Ask for dances and reactions (“do a dance,” “react to that”). They are for fun, they stay out of anything he sends for you, and you can tell them to go away.

Li Laoshi, Mandarin conversation practice

Start speaking Chinese, or say “let’s practice Chinese,” and Li Laoshi, a Mandarin tutor, takes over with his own voice. He speaks slower, adapts to your level, and keeps a log of how you are doing.

Edwin, the background assistant

For long jobs (“research the best laptops under 800 dollars and write me a summary”) Jarvis says “passing it to my assistant” and hands the job to Edwin. You keep talking while a progress tile shows on screen. Jobs survive restarts, and he announces the result out loud.

  • “What’s Edwin working on?”
  • “Tell Edwin to also include prices.”
  • “Cancel that job.”

Memory

Say “remember that I’m allergic to peanuts” and it sticks from day to day, on your Mac only. He keeps a day log, a running-jokes list and topic files with an index, and starts every conversation by reading them.

  • See it: “what do you remember about me?”
  • Delete it: “forget the peanuts thing” (he asks first), or “forget everything.”
  • The files are plain text in the Jarvis folder and in ~/.claude/projects/; open them any time.

Your phone

Open the same Jarvis, with the same memory and connectors, from your phone’s browser: a mic button or a text box that talks to your Mac. On the same Wi‑Fi it works right away behind a PIN; with a free Tailscale account it works from anywhere, privately, with no certificate warnings. Steps are in the README inside the download. JARVIS_PHONE=0 turns it off.

Type instead of talk

In a library or on a call? Click Jarvis’s Terminal window, type a line, press Return. No wake word needed. Shortcuts: /mute and /unmute (stop and start listening), /reset (fresh conversation), /status, /help, /exit.

Safety

He asks before anything leaves your Mac or cannot be undone: sending email or texts, posting, buying, deleting. A built-in guard also blocks dangerous shell commands. Answer “yes” to go ahead.

Settings

Everything lives in the .env file in the Jarvis folder (.env.example lists every option). Jarvis restarts himself when it changes.

JARVIS_USER_NAME / JARVIS_USER_CITY / JARVIS_USER_TZYour name, your city (for weather) and time zone.
ELEVENLABS_API_KEYA nicer, more natural voice from ElevenLabs (free tier is about 20 minutes of speech a month). Without it he uses the built-in Kokoro voice. voice_check.py tests the key.
JARVIS_MODELhaiku (quickest), sonnet (balanced, default) or opus (smartest).
JARVIS_HUD=0No on-screen display.
JARVIS_PHONE=0No phone page.
JARVIS_INTRO=0Never play the spoken welcome. Delete .intro_done to hear it again.

Privacy

Listening for the wake word and turning your voice into text both happen on your Mac. Only the text of what you say goes to Claude, along with whatever you ask him to read from your connected accounts. Your settings, keys, memory and phone PIN stay in files on your Mac and nowhere else. The download contains no personal data. If you add an ElevenLabs key, the lines he speaks go to ElevenLabs to be voiced.

Troubleshooting

He doesn’t answer, or says Claude isn’t logged in.
Run claude in Terminal, sign in with a Pro or Max account, then /exit and relaunch Jarvis.
He never hears “Hey Jarvis.”
System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone: make sure Terminal is on. Speak a little closer, then wait for the tink before your command. Type /status in his window to see whether he is muted.
Setup stalls or the download looks stuck.
The speech models are about 1.5 GB; on a slow connection that takes a while. If it fails, run setup again: it skips what already downloaded.
He says he can’t see my Gmail, Drive, Calendar or Spotify.
Connect the missing one in claude.ai › Settings › Connectors, then type /reset. Run claude mcp list in Terminal to see what is connected.
Reminders or Calendar do nothing.
macOS asks for permission the first time. If you clicked Don’t Allow, turn them on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Reminders / Calendars.
Spotify doesn’t play.
Open the Spotify app first, and make sure the Spotify connector is connected.
The display doesn’t open.
It needs Google Chrome. Or turn it off with JARVIS_HUD=0; he works fine without it.
macOS won’t open the file.
Right-click it, choose Open, then Open again. Or run chmod +x Jarvis.command setup.sh in the folder.
Anything else.
Say “Jarvis, what’s wrong?”, type /status, and look at jarvis.log in the Jarvis folder. The full text of the tour is GETTING_STARTED.md in the download.
